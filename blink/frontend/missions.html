Guided missions

Learn to read SPHEREx, one target at a time

Five short missions on the challenge targets. Each one teaches a tool of the viewer, asks you to find the object yourself, and then checks your answer against the catalogue and the measurements.

How a mission works

Open the field

Follow the steps

Mark the object

Click the source you think it is. The viewer measures it for you.

File a report

Compare with the data

Field report

Words used in the viewer

Visit (epoch)

SPHEREx scans the whole sky every six months. All exposures of one spot taken close together in time are averaged into one image per visit.

Band (detector D1–D6)

SPHEREx has six detectors, each covering part of 0.75–5 µm. D1 is the shortest wavelength, D6 the longest. Cold objects glow most in D5–D6.

102 channels

A filter that changes colour across each detector splits the range into 102 spectral channels, 17 per band. Every exposure sees a spot at one of them.

Blink

The visits shown one after another. A source that moved jumps; one that changed brightness flickers.

Difference

One visit minus another. Unchanged stars cancel; a moved source leaves a blue–red pair, a brightened one a single spot.

Stack

All visits averaged: the deepest view, where faint sources stand out from the noise.

Proper motion

How fast a star crosses the sky, in arcseconds per year. Only nearby objects move enough for SPHEREx to see.

Arcsecond (″)

1/3600 of a degree. One SPHEREx pixel spans 6.15″, about the width of a coin seen from 600 metres.

Light curve

Brightness against time. Pulsating and dust-hidden stars show rises and dips.

σ (sigma)

The noise level. A change of 5σ or more is very unlikely to be noise.
